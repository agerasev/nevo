# First million-tick run

**November 5, 2026.** Run `runs/2026-11-05_18-42-40`, seed
`12981673030976906931`, revision `b86d5bd`: 1,000,897 ticks in one invocation,
starting with 20 animals and 500 ripe plants, with immigration floor 20.
The complete metrics log contains 10,009 consecutive windows, including the
final partial window. Population changes match births + immigration − deaths
in every window. Derived analysis stays beside the checkpoints in `analysis/`.

## Measured results

| Measurement | Ticks 0–20,000 | Ticks 900,000–1,000,000 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mean population, all ticks | 22.69 | 25.01 |
| Immigrants per 10,000 ticks | 564.5 | 0.6 |
| Births / deaths | 38.8% | 99.7% |
| Food energy gained per tick | 39.73 | 49.99 |
| Mean plant energy, sampled window endpoints | 198.81 | 64.56 |

Early improvement was fragile: the population returned to the immigration floor
around ticks 10,000–13,000. After tick 20,000, births replaced over 99% of deaths;
the longest recorded interval without immigration was 61,500 ticks
(194,800–256,300]. Immigration remained enabled and occasionally contributed.
There were 25,003 births, 26,313 deaths, and 1,316 immigrants overall, ending
with 26 animals.

The final checkpoint contains no unmodified random immigrant genomes: every
speed-output bias is 1.345–1.473, outside the initialization range [−0.5, 0.5).
Reconstructing the last actions from saved recurrent memory gives commanded
speed fractions 0.904–0.991; one animal's direction falls within the movement
dead zone. This is a snapshot, not a measured behavioral trend.

## Controller probes

Probes of all 26 final brains using the original revision's equations indicate
fast, biased food-gradient following. Across 360 fixed gradient directions at
the checkpoint's median food strength (0.303), 99.87% of moving settled responses
pointed up-gradient; mean heading error was 26.8° and median speed was 98.2% of
maximum. Two genetic groups (9 and 17 animals) share this rule with different
directional biases. Zero-gradient probes retained fast drift.

Recurrence contributes to steering: clearing memory every tick substantially
worsened alignment. Reversals usually settled within a few ticks, and no
sustained oscillation or multiple fixed-input attractors appeared in the tested
grid. Within the checkpoint's observed strength range, food strength modulated
direction and speed more weakly than the gradient.
These are controller responses, not measured fitness effects or a proof that
memory has no other role. Scripts, numerical validation against Rust f32, weight
matrices, and figures are in the run's `analysis/` directory; see its
`rnn-interpretation.md` for methods and limits.

## Interpretation and next comparison

The late population approaches the food-production ceiling: 500 plants grow
by at most 1 energy per tick, feeding transfers 10%, and each animal consumes
2 per tick. That supports approximately 25 animals. Near-total use of this
supply explains the population plateau; it does not establish evolutionary
stagnation. Mean plant energy settled near 64 after roughly 300,000 ticks.

This run provides evidence of improved foraging and near self-sufficiency,
with inherited mutated controllers present at the end. It does not isolate
genetic adaptation from ecological change and selection among random arrivals,
or establish robustness across seeds. Historical movement, brain, and diversity
metrics were disabled.

The next controlled comparison should evaluate final and fresh random genomes
in matched fresh worlds, with equal starting energy, reset recurrent memory,
and immigration disabled. A separate continuation without immigration would
test persistence of the existing ecosystem.
